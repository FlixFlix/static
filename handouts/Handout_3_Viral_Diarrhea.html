Viral Diarrhea (Stomach Bug)

Parent & Caregiver Education Handout

🔍 What Is It?

Viral diarrhea — also called a "stomach bug" — happens when a virus (like rotavirus or norovirus) irritates your child's stomach and intestines. It causes loose, watery stools, and sometimes vomiting. It is very contagious and usually spreads through hand-to-mouth contact. The good news: most cases get better on their own in 3–7 days with proper care at home.

⚠️ Common Signs & Symptoms

  • Frequent, watery, or loose stools
  • Vomiting (often starts before the diarrhea)
  • Low-grade fever
  • Belly cramps or pain
  • Loss of appetite
  • Tiredness and fussiness
  • Signs of dehydration (see "When Should I Call" below)

🚨 When Should I Call My Child's Provider?

Call right away if your child has:

  • No wet diaper in 6+ hours (or no urination in 8+ hours for older kids)
  • No tears when crying / dry mouth or lips
  • Sunken eyes or sunken soft spot (in babies)
  • Blood or mucus in the stool
  • High fever (over 102 °F / 39 °C)
  • Unable to keep any fluids down
  • Extreme sleepiness, limpness, or hard to wake up

🏡 Home Care

  • Keep your child hydrated! This is the #1 priority
  • Use an oral rehydration solution (like Pedialyte®) — give small, frequent sips
  • If vomiting: offer 1–2 teaspoons every 2–3 minutes, then slowly increase
  • Keep breastfeeding or formula-feeding — do not stop
  • Return to normal foods as soon as your child can eat (no need to "starve" the illness)
  • Avoid juice, soda, and sports drinks — too much sugar can make diarrhea worse

💊 Medications

  • No antibiotics — antibiotics do NOT work against viruses
  • No anti-diarrhea medicines (like Imodium®) for young children — these can be dangerous
  • Your provider may prescribe ondansetron (Zofran®) to stop vomiting so your child can drink
  • Use acetaminophen or ibuprofen for fever or discomfort (follow provider's dosing)
  • Avoid aspirin or bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol®) in children due to the risk of Reye's syndrome

🛡️ Prevention Tips

  • Handwashing! Scrub with soap and water for at least 20 seconds
  • Make sure your baby gets the rotavirus vaccine on schedule
  • Clean and disinfect surfaces (especially bathrooms, changing tables, and toys)
  • Keep sick children home from school or daycare until diarrhea-free for 24 hours
  • Wash soiled clothing and linens in hot water
  • Teach kids: "Wash your hands after the potty and before eating!"

⭐ Fun Family Tip — "When to Call" Fridge Checklist

Write the warning signs from the red box above on a colorful card and stick it on the refrigerator with a magnet. That way, any caregiver (parent, grandparent, babysitter) can quickly check the list and know when to call the doctor — even at 2 a.m. Add your provider's phone number at the bottom. When in doubt, call it out! 📞
