Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

Parent & Caregiver Education Handout

🔍 What Is It?

A UTI happens when germs (usually bacteria) get into your child's urinary system — the bladder, kidneys, or the tubes that carry urine. It is very common in children and is not caused by poor parenting. Girls get UTIs more often, but boys can get them too — especially uncircumcised boys under age 1.

⚠️ Common Signs & Symptoms

  • Pain or burning during urination
  • Feeling the need to "go" often, but only a little comes out
  • Cloudy, smelly, or bloody urine
  • Fever (sometimes the only sign in babies)
  • Belly or lower-back pain
  • Fussiness, poor feeding, or new daytime wetting in a potty-trained child

🚨 When Should I Call My Child's Provider?

Call right away if your child has:

  • Fever over 100.4 °F (38.0 °C) in a baby under 3 months, or over 101 °F (38.3 °C) in an older child
  • Severe back or side pain
  • Vomiting and unable to keep fluids down
  • Blood in the urine
  • No improvement after 48 hours of antibiotics
  • Symptoms return after finishing medicine

🏡 Home Care

  • Offer extra fluids during the infection — water is best
  • Encourage your child to use the bathroom every 2–3 hours (no "holding it" while sick)
  • Use a warm washcloth on the belly or lower back for comfort
  • Dress your child in loose, comfortable clothing and cotton underwear
  • Avoid bubble baths and scented soaps near the genital area
  • Let your child rest — the body heals faster with sleep

💊 Medications

  • Your provider will prescribe an antibiotic (such as amoxicillin-clavulanate or cephalexin)
  • Finish ALL doses — even if your child feels better!
  • Most uncomplicated UTIs need 3–7 days of medicine
  • Measure liquid antibiotics with an oral dosing syringe — never a household kitchen spoon
  • Give acetaminophen or ibuprofen for pain or fever (follow provider's dosing)
  • Never give leftover antibiotics from a previous illness

🛡️ Prevention Tips

  • Teach your child to always wipe front to back
  • Keep your child well-hydrated every day — make water the go-to drink
  • Encourage regular bathroom breaks — no "holding it in"
  • Treat constipation (hard poop presses on the bladder!)
  • Choose breathable cotton underwear and avoid tight clothing
  • Change diapers promptly and clean the diaper area front to back
💧

⭐ Fun Family Tip — "Hydration Challenge Chart"

Make a colorful chart and hang it on the fridge! Each time your child finishes a glass of water, they add a sticker 🌟. Set a daily goal (for example, 6 glasses). When they hit the goal, they earn a small reward. Staying hydrated keeps the urinary system flushed and helps prevent new infections. Make it a family challenge — everyone drinks up!
