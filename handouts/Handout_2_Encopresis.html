Encopresis (Stool Soiling)

Parent & Caregiver Education Handout

🔍 What Is It?

Encopresis is when a child who is already potty-trained leaks or passes stool in their underwear without meaning to. It is almost always caused by chronic constipation. Hard, backed-up stool stretches the rectum over time, so the child loses the feeling of needing to go. Softer stool then leaks around the blockage. Your child is NOT doing this on purpose. It is a medical condition, not a behavior problem.

⚠️ Common Signs & Symptoms

  • Stool stains or smears in underwear
  • Large or painful bowel movements (or avoiding the toilet)
  • Going many days without a bowel movement
  • Belly pain, bloating, or poor appetite
  • Hiding during bowel movements or avoiding the bathroom
  • Urine accidents (the full rectum presses on the bladder)

🚨 When Should I Call My Child's Provider?

Call the provider if your child has:

  • No bowel movement for 5 or more days
  • Severe belly pain or vomiting
  • Swollen, hard belly that does not go down
  • Blood in the stool (not from a small anal tear)
  • Ribbon-like stools or trouble passing gas
  • Soiling that continues after 2–3 months of treatment
  • Emotional distress, embarrassment, or school avoidance
  • Weight loss or poor growth

🏡 Home Care

  • Schedule toilet sits for 5–10 minutes after meals (the body naturally wants to "go" after eating)
  • Use a footstool so knees are higher than hips — this helps the body push
  • Offer plenty of water, fruits, vegetables, and whole grains
  • Never punish accidents — stay calm and supportive
  • Praise effort ("Great job sitting on the potty!"), not just results
  • Keep a stool diary to track bowel movements

💊 Medications

  • Step 1 — "Clean-out": Your provider may prescribe a higher dose of MiraLAX® (PEG 3350) for 3–6 days to clear the backed-up stool
  • Step 2 — Daily maintenance: A smaller daily dose of MiraLAX® to keep stools soft
  • How to give it: Mix the powder completely in 4–8 oz of water, juice, or milk until dissolved; store all laxatives out of children's reach
  • The goal is soft, painless poops every day
  • Treatment often lasts 6 months or longer — do not stop early!
  • Your provider will slowly lower the dose over time

🛡️ Prevention Tips

  • Serve high-fiber foods daily (fruits, veggies, beans, oatmeal)
  • Keep your child well-hydrated with water throughout the day
  • Encourage active play and exercise
  • Stick to regular toilet-sit times — even when things are going well
  • Don't rush potty time — let your child relax
  • Talk openly: "Everyone poops — and sometimes our bodies need a little help!"
📅

⭐ Fun Family Tip — "Potty Reward Calendar"

Hang a calendar in the bathroom. Every time your child sits on the toilet after a meal (even without a bowel movement), they earn a sticker 🌟. After collecting a set number of stickers (for example, 5), they pick a reward — a favorite snack, extra story time, or a small toy. Focus on sitting and trying, not on results. This takes the pressure off and builds a healthy routine! Celebrate the effort — the results will follow.
